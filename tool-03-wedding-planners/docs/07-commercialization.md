# Commercialization

No pricing is set. Budgets in this niche are small; the Field Lab strategy treats this as a teaching and audience tool.

## Target user
Owner or lead planner at a planning company with two or more planners; destination wedding planners.

## Buyer
The owner.

## Business problem
Bookings lost quietly to slow replies and missed follow-ups, and referrals never asked for. How much this costs is **not measured**; it would be measured with a real planner as median first-reply time, quotes followed up on schedule, and the share of bookings from referrals.

## Implementation
Set `config/rules.json` (team, weddings per date, timings, channels, templates, brand). Import past enquiries from a spreadsheet or CRM export. Agree how DMs and WhatsApp get logged.

## White-label opportunity
Another consultant changes one config file and the sample data; no code changes.

## Service opportunity
Small setup and a follow-up rules review; possibly a monthly check of response times. Only if planners confirm the problem.

## Product opportunity
Unlikely on its own: HoneyBook and Aisle Planner own this space. The capacity-by-date rule could be pitched to them, or used as a lead-in for a larger custom build.

## Pricing value drivers
Number of planners and weddings a year · average planning fee · how many enquiries arrive through channels without an API · whether they already pay for a CRM.
