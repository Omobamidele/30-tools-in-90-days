# Phase 1 — Industry Research: B2B SaaS (mid-market)

**Researched:** 2026-10-07
**Evidence labels:**
- **[V]** Verified from a named primary source (survey or benchmark publisher, official vendor documentation, or peer-reviewed or HBR research).
- **[V2]** Widely cited, but read through a secondary page because the primary is paywalled or binary.
- **[W]** Weak: a vendor blog or marketing statistic. Used for direction only, never for numbers we rely on.
- **[A]** Assumption, to be checked in Phase 2 or in interviews.

---

## 0. Scope: which business are we building for?

"B2B SaaS (mid-market)" could mean two different companies. This research takes the first.

| Reading | Who the system serves | Used here? |
|---|---|---|
| **A SaaS vendor whose customers are mid-market companies.** Typically $10M–$150M ARR, with an average contract of about $25K–$100K a year | The vendor's own go-to-market team: sales, customer success, RevOps and finance | **Yes** |
| A mid-market company that *buys* SaaS (SaaS spend management) | IT and procurement | No. It's a crowded category (Vendr, Zylo, Sastrify), and it's a different buyer |

**What "mid-market" means:**
- **[V] Gartner's definition:** midsize enterprises have $50M–$1B revenue and/or 100–1,000 employees. Gartner itself calls this cut "vendor driven" and "arbitrary". The segment is defined mostly by limited IT staff, skills and funding (Gartner IT Glossary).
- **[V] ICONIQ's report bands** by average contract value (ACV). The most common band among respondents is **$100K–$500K (42%)**, then $50K–$100K (22%) and $10K–$50K (20%).
- **[V2]** Benchmark write-ups commonly treat a **$25K–$100K ACV** as mid-market.

**Working definition:** a SaaS vendor with $20M–$150M ARR, selling contracts of $25K–$150K a year to companies with 100–1,000 employees, through sales-led or hybrid motions. **[A]**

## 1. Business model

- **Recurring subscription revenue.** The vendor sells annual or multi-year contracts and recognises revenue over the term. Growth comes from new customers *and* from existing customers expanding, minus churn and downgrades.
- **[V] Pricing is moving towards hybrid and usage-based models.** About 48% of companies use hybrid pricing as their primary model in 2025–26, and usage/consumption and outcome pricing are rising (ICONIQ, *State of GTM 2026*, p.10).
- **[V] Contracts are getting shorter.** New-customer contracts under one year rose sharply between 2023 and 2026: customers are "signing faster, but for shorter durations" (ICONIQ 2026, p.24). Shorter terms mean **more renewal events per customer**.
- **[V] Retention carries the economics.**
  - Private SaaS gross retention is approaching 90% (86% in 2023), and net retention stays above 100% (KeyBanc Capital Markets & Sapphire Ventures, *2025 SaaS Survey*, press release).
  - Top-quartile net dollar retention is about 110–120% across revenue bands (ICONIQ 2026, p.17).
  - Bessemer's operating bars for net retention are **100% good, 110% better, 120%+ best**.
- **[V] Sales pay is shifting to retention.** Account executives (AEs) are increasingly paid on **net new recurring revenue and net dollar retention**, not just bookings (ICONIQ 2026, p.14). The organisation is being re-anchored on revenue from existing customers.
- **[V2] Mid-market net retention:** about 108% median for $25K–$100K ACV, versus about 118% for enterprise (benchmark summaries citing ChartMogul and others). Treat the exact figures as directional.

## 2. Customer types (the vendor's customers)

- **[A]** Mid-market companies of 100–1,000 employees in any vertical. They have a small IT team and a functional buyer (head of marketing, operations, finance or support).
- **[V] They are cautious about lock-in.** Smaller buyers have "more variable" usage and are "more sensitive to getting locked in" (ICONIQ 2026, p.11, quoting Procore's former CRO). That supports shorter terms and pay-as-you-go pricing.

## 3. Buyer types (who would buy an *internal system* at the vendor)

| Role | Cares about | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **CRO / VP Sales** | Pipeline coverage, win rate, forecast accuracy, quota attainment | **[V]** Pipeline coverage dipped from 3.9x to 3.6x (ICONIQ 2025); 62% of ramped AEs hit quota in 2025 (ICONIQ 2026, p.25) |
| **VP Customer Success** | Gross and net retention, renewals, expansion, accounts per CSM | **[V]** Customer success (CS) spend runs about 3–8% of revenue (Gainsight CS Index 2025, 400+ companies) |
| **Head of RevOps** | Data quality, routing, reporting, tool sprawl | **[V]** 71% say poor data quality hurts GTM; only 11% rate their data as excellent (Openprise, *2025 State of RevOps Data Quality*) |
| **CFO** | Net retention, CAC payback, forecast vs actual, billing accuracy | **[V]** Payback on AE cost expected to fall to about 18 months by 2026 (KeyBanc/Sapphire 2025) |

**[A] Likely economic buyer:** the CRO or CFO. **Likely champion:** the head of RevOps, who owns the tools and the data plumbing.

## 4. Acquisition channels

- **[V] Pipeline sources** (ICONIQ 2026, p.18): sales-sourced pipeline is the largest share. High-growth companies get about 60–80% of pipeline from sales and channel versus about 15–20% from marketing.
- **[V] Win rate by opportunity source** (ICONIQ 2026, p.21): **customer-success-sourced deals win about 52%**, against about 38–43% for sales and about 23–27% for marketing.

  > **Selling into existing customers is the most efficient channel, but it depends on CS spotting and handing over the opportunity.**
- **[V] Cost per lead and per opportunity:** about $500–$800 per lead and about $6,300–$8,000 per opportunity for mid-market/enterprise (ICONIQ 2026, p.22).
- **[V] Bottom-up motions are growing.** High-growth companies expect about 20% of revenue from self-serve versus about 10% for peers. Self-serve is used to reach user-level buyers, then "drive a top-down motion" (ICONIQ 2026, p.9, quoting Snowflake's former CRO). This creates **product-usage signals that sales has to act on**.

## 5. Sales motion

- **[V] Mostly sales-led:** in 2026, 62% of companies are sales-led, 31% hybrid and 7% product-led (ICONIQ 2026, p.4).
- **[V] Funnel conversion rates** (ICONIQ 2026, p.19):

  | Stage | Conversion |
  |---|---|
  | Lead → MQL | ~25% |
  | MQL → SQL | ~27% |
  | SQL → closed-won | ~25% |
  | Demo → closed-won | ~36% |
  | Free trial / proof of concept → paid | ~50% |
- **[V] Sales cycles:**
  - about 12 weeks for $10K–$50K ACV, 17 weeks for $50K–$100K, and 24 weeks for $100K+ (ICONIQ 2026, p.23)
  - overall average down about 6 weeks in 2025
- **[V] Team structure:**
  - **SDRs:** 1 SDR to 2.4 AEs; SDR ramp of 3.0 months, tenure of 1.9 years, and only 60% at quota (The Bridge Group, *2025 SDR Report*, 351 companies)
  - **Proofs of concept:** mostly supported by a shared solutions architect for $50K–$100K deals (ICONIQ 2026, p.20)
- **[V] Discounts and deal desk:** discounts beyond set thresholds are routed for approval by size, from team lead up to VP or CFO, usually in CPQ software (Salesforce, *What is a Deal Desk*; Salesforce CPQ documentation).
- **[V] Rep time:** sellers spend **40% of their time selling**. The rest goes to admin, data entry, internal meetings and research (Salesforce, *State of Sales 2026*, 4,050 sellers in 22 countries).

## 6. Customer journey (vendor's view)

```
Lead/sign-up → qualify (SDR) → discovery/demo (AE) → POC/trial → proposal + discount approval
  → contract signed → handoff to onboarding/CS → implementation → adoption
    → health monitoring → expansion opportunities (seats, usage, modules)
      → renewal (notice window, price change, true-up) → renew / downsell / churn
```

- **[V] Response time on inbound leads matters.**
  - In an audit of 2,241 US firms, only 37% responded to a web lead within an hour, and the average first response was 42 hours.
  - Firms that responded within an hour were about 7x more likely to qualify the lead (Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, *HBR*, March 2011).
  - **[V2]** The figures were read via secondary citations, because the HBR article is paywalled.
- **[V] Renewals are often late:** **27–29% of renewals are late** (ICONIQ 2026, p.30, "% of late renewals" by AI adoption level).
- **[W] Onboarding effects:**
  - poor onboarding is associated with higher first-year churn
  - a slow first CS contact after signing hurts satisfaction

  These come from vendor-published stats, with direction plausible but numbers unconfirmed.
- **[W] Renewal notice periods:** auto-renewal clauses commonly need 30–90 days' notice, and mid-market renewals are typically worked 60–90 days out. These come from buyer-side vendor blogs; Phase 2 should confirm them against real contract templates.

## 7. Operational workflow (who does what)

| Stage | Owner | Work | Systems |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead capture and routing | Marketing ops / RevOps | Dedupe, match lead to account, assign owner, set SLA | CRM + routing tool |
| Qualification | SDR | Outreach, discovery, book meeting | Sales engagement, CRM |
| Opportunity | AE (+ solutions architect) | Discovery, demo, POC, proposal | CRM, call recording, CPQ |
| Deal desk | Deal desk / finance / legal | Discount and term approvals, non-standard clauses | CPQ, e-signature, CLM |
| Close → handoff | AE → CSM / onboarding | Transfer goals, stakeholders, promises, success criteria | CRM fields, docs, Slack |
| Onboarding | Onboarding / implementation | Kickoff, configuration, training, first value | Project tool, CS platform |
| Adoption and health | CSM | Usage review, QBRs, risk flags | Product analytics, CS platform, support desk |
| Expansion | CSM → AE / account manager | Spot signal, qualify, create opportunity | CRM, product analytics |
| Renewal | CSM / account manager / renewals rep | Forecast, price uplift, true-up, paperwork, notice dates | CRM, billing, CLM |
| Billing and collections | Finance | Invoices, usage billing, dunning | Billing system, ERP |

**[A]** Ownership of renewals and expansion varies by company: CS, account managers or a dedicated renewals team. That variation is itself a configuration requirement.

## 8. Common systems

**[A]** These are the typical categories for a $20–150M ARR vendor. The products are named to show the category; we have not measured their market share.
- **CRM:** Salesforce, HubSpot
- **Sales engagement:** Outreach, Salesloft, HubSpot Sequences
- **Conversation intelligence:** Gong, Chorus
- **Routing and scheduling:** LeanData, Chili Piper
- **Forecasting:** Clari, or CRM-native forecasting
- **CPQ and contracts:** Salesforce CPQ, DealHub, PandaDoc, DocuSign, Ironclad
- **CS platforms:** Gainsight, ChurnZero, Vitally, Planhat
- **Product analytics:** Pendo, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment
- **Support:** Zendesk, Intercom
- **Billing:** Stripe Billing, Chargebee, Maxio; NetSuite as ERP
- **Data:** a warehouse (Snowflake or BigQuery) plus reverse ETL for the more mature companies

**[V] Disconnected systems are a known blocker:** 51% of sales leaders say disconnected systems slow their AI initiatives (Salesforce, *State of Sales 2026*).

## 9. Manual processes (where people still work by hand)

1. **Handoff from sale to CS.** Context moves through CRM notes, a Slack message or a call. Promises made in the sale are lost. **[W]** for numbers, **[A]** for frequency.
2. **Renewal preparation.** Pulling contract terms (dates, notice period, uplift cap, committed seats or usage) from PDFs, checking actual usage and billing, and building a renewal quote. **[A]**, supported by **[V]** the 27–29% late-renewal rate.
3. **Expansion spotting.** CSMs eyeball usage dashboards. Usage over the commitment, and new teams adopting the product, are noticed late. **[A]**, supported by **[V]** CS-sourced deals winning about 52% and roundtable operators crediting "formal CSQL programs" and "product telemetry" (ICONIQ 2026, p.21).
4. **Usage true-ups and overages.** Reconciling committed vs actual usage between the product, billing and the CRM. **[V]** Running pay-as-you-go "takes real operational maturity… billing infrastructure, usage instrumentation, and forecasting discipline" (ICONIQ 2026, p.11).
5. **Discount approvals** outside CPQ, done by email or Slack. **[A]**
6. **Data hygiene.** **[V]** 74% of sellers prioritise data cleansing (Salesforce 2026), and 71% say poor data hurts GTM (Openprise 2025).
7. **Forecast roll-ups and board reporting.** Spreadsheets reconciling CRM, billing and CS data. **[A]**

## 10. Data sources the vendor already holds

- **CRM:** accounts, contacts, opportunities, stages, amounts, close dates, owners.
- **Contracts:** term, start and end dates, auto-renew, notice period, uplift cap, committed quantity, overage rate, termination rights.
- **Billing:** subscriptions, invoices, payments, usage records, MRR.
- **Product telemetry:** active users, seats used versus purchased, feature adoption, usage against the commitment.
- **Support:** ticket volume, severity, sentiment.
- **Calls and email:** recordings, transcripts, stakeholder activity.

**[A]** The data mostly exists. **It sits in five to eight systems that don't agree on account IDs, dates or amounts.**

## 11. Important business events

| Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Inbound lead or sign-up from a target account | Speed to first response drives qualification (HBR 2011) |
| Trial or POC started or ended | About 50% conversion to paid; outcomes depend on how it's run (ICONIQ 2026) |
| Deal closed | Starts the handoff clock and onboarding |
| First value reached / onboarding stalled | Early churn risk |
| Usage passes commitment (or drops) | Expansion opportunity or churn risk; drives overage billing |
| Champion leaves / new stakeholder appears | Renewal risk or expansion path |
| Support ticket spike | Risk signal |
| Renewal notice date | The last date the customer can cancel; the date the vendor must have the renewal ready |
| Renewal date | Revenue renews, downsells or churns |
| Invoice overdue | Collections, and often a sign of churn risk |

## 12. Likely bottlenecks (hypotheses for Phase 2)

1. **Renewals are worked late and without contract facts.**
   - **[V]** 27–29% of renewals are late (ICONIQ 2026).
   - Shorter contracts multiply renewal events.
   - Notice dates and uplift terms sit in PDFs and contract tools, apart from the usage and billing data needed to price the renewal.
2. **Expansion signals are seen late, or not acted on.**
   - **[V]** CS-sourced opportunities win most often (about 52%).
   - The signal sits in product telemetry and billing, while the opportunity has to be created in the CRM by a different team.
3. **Committed vs actual usage isn't reconciled.**
   - **[V]** Hybrid and usage pricing is rising (about 48% hybrid as primary model), and pay-as-you-go needs mature billing and forecasting.
   - **[A]** Overages go unbilled, and under-use isn't flagged before renewal.
4. **The handoff from sale to CS loses context.**
   - **[W]** for scale.
   - **[A]** Promises and success criteria from the sale don't reach the CSM, which makes onboarding slow and churn risk unseen.
5. **Inbound leads are routed and answered too slowly.**
   - **[V]** (HBR 2011).
   - **[A]** Mid-market vendors without LeanData or Chili Piper rely on round-robin and manual matching.
   - Note that routing tools exist and are fairly cheap, which weakens this one against the "$30 tool test".
6. **CRM data quality undermines forecasts and every automation above.** **[V]** (Openprise 2025; Salesforce 2026).

## 13. The "$30 tool" warning for this industry

This is the most tool-saturated industry in the challenge.
- **Off-the-shelf tools exist for each single function:** Gainsight, ChurnZero and Vitally (CS); Clari (forecast); LeanData (routing); Gong (calls); Chargebee (billing).
- **Phase 2 has to find work that falls between tools.** That means the cross-system logic tied to *this* vendor's contracts, pricing and team structure:
  - contract terms combined with usage and billing at renewal
  - signals that must cross from CS to sales
  - commitment true-ups
- **[A]** Mid-market vendors often can't justify Gainsight plus Clari plus a CLM plus the admin staff to connect them. A focused internal system can fill that gap. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
